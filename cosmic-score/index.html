What is the Cosmic Score?

Most astrology sites tell you what one tradition says about you. Mythsensus computes 26 traditions in parallel and reports their consensus as a single number from 1 to 1,000. A high score means most systems agree on the same archetype; a middling score means the traditions disagree — and that disagreement is itself information about you.

The score is not a verdict. It is the sound 26 ancient traditions make when asked the same question about you on the same day.

How to read your score

The Cosmic Score does not say you are a "good" person or a "lucky" person. It says how loudly 26 traditions agree on the same archetype for the moment you were born. A 940 means almost every tradition reads you the same way — your archetype is unusually legible. A 480 means the traditions argue — you carry contradictions; that is its own gift.

Tiers (approximate)

From low to high: Common · Uncommon · Rare · Epic · Legendary · Mythic. The deity drawn for your daily blessing follows the same tier system; high-tier users draw legendary and mythic deities more often.

How are the 26 weights decided?

Each tradition's sub-score is weighted by its signal-to-noise on the same chart. Systems that produce highly granular answers across birth dates — Vedic with its 27 nakshatras, BaZi with 60 stem-branch pairs, Mayan with 260 day-signs — contribute more granularity than systems with coarser bins (Western astrology with 12 sun signs, Biorhythm with 3 cycles). The weights are calibrated once across thousands of historical charts and then frozen.

Crucially, the weights do not change based on who you are. A Thai user, an Indian user, and a Norwegian user all get the same Cosmic Score from the same birth date. Mythsensus does not "favour" the tradition you grew up with — your cultural background is not an input. Same input → same Cosmic Score, anywhere in the world.

How is the Cosmic Score calculated?

Each of the 26 systems is computed independently from your birth date (and where available, birth time and birth city). Each contributes a normalised sub-score; the Cosmic Score is the weighted consensus across all 26. When most systems agree, the score is high; when they disagree, the score reflects that uncertainty rather than averaging it away.
