Zoroastrianism is one of the world's oldest living religions, and by most scholarly accounts, the first monotheistic faith in human history. At its theological core, Zoroastrianism posits a universe engaged in a cosmic war between two fundamental principles: Ahura Mazda (Wise Lord), the supreme deity of light, truth, and order, and Angra Mainyu (Destructive Spirit, later called Ahriman), the force of darkness, deception, and chaos. Every human being is born into this war and must choose a side through their thoughts, words, and deeds.

They were understood as participants in the war. Each constellation, each bright star, had a divine patron â€” a yazata (a being worthy of worship, a divine being subordinate to Ahura Mazda) or an agent of Ahriman. They were the keepers of Avestan tradition, the guardians of the sacred fire, and the interpreters of divine will written in the stars. When Alexander the Great conquered Persia in 330 BCE, the collision between Magi star-lore and Greek philosophical tradition produced one of history's great intellectual explosions: the birth of Hellenistic astrology as we know it.

Zarathustra was born into a world already ancient with tradition, and from the beginning, the cosmos recognized him. The legends recorded in Zoroastrian texts say that at his birth, the star Sirius blazed with unusual brightness. Plants budded out of season. Animals grew gentle. Vohu Manah led him to Ahura Mazda himself. There, in a vision of overwhelming light and clarity, Zarathustra received the cosmic revelation that would reshape the religious history of the world: existence is a moral conflict. Light and darkness are not metaphors. They are real forces engaged in a real battle, and the choices of every human consciousness are the decisive weapons in that battle. Core Philosophy

Zoroastrian astrology is unique among ancient systems in that it is explicitly moral. The stars do not merely describe personality or predict events. They map the soul's alignment within the cosmic war between Asha and Druj. A birth chart in this system is not simply a snapshot of celestial positions â€” it is a reading of which divine and demonic forces were most active at the moment of a soul's entry into the physical world, and therefore what spiritual challenges and divine aids that soul will encounter on its journey.

The Zoroastrian contribution to the global astrological tradition is immense and often underappreciated: through the Persian Magi, the ancient star-wisdom of the Iranian plateau merged with Greek philosophical precision to produce horoscopic astrology itself. Without Zoroastrianism, there may have been no Hellenistic astrology. Without Hellenistic astrology, no Arabic Parts, no medieval European astrology, no modern practice.
