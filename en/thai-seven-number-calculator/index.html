What the nine bases are

The nine bases (ฐาน) the numbers are laid across are Attha (อัตตะ, the self), Hina (หินะ, weakness), Thanang (ธนัง, wealth), Pita (ปิตา, father), Mata (มาตา, mother), Phoka (โภคา, possessions), Matchima (มัชฌิมา, the middle path), Tanu (ตะนุ, the body) and Kamma (กัมมะ, action). Seven numbers, nine bases — the grid is what a Thai reader looks at first.

The Thai 7-Number System emerged from the confluence of two ancient rivers of knowledge: Indian Brahmin numerology and Southeast Asian Buddhist cosmology. When Brahmanism spread into the Khmer Empire and eventually into the Thai kingdoms of Sukhothai and Ayutthaya — roughly 700 years ago — it carried with it a complete numerical theology. Numbers were not abstract. They were alive. Each carried a planetary lord, a guardian deity, and a vibrational signature that could be read against a human life.

What makes เลข ๗ ตัว ๙ ฐาน distinctive is its mathematical foundation: it operates in base 9 rather than the base 10 of modern arithmetic. This is not an accident or an eccentricity. The number 9 held supreme cosmological significance in Thai-Brahmin thought — nine sacred gems (นพรัตน์), nine divine mountains, nine levels of heaven. To calculate in base 9 was to align human reckoning with the architecture of the universe itself.

The system generates seven distinct numbers from two sources: the numerical values of the syllables in a person's full Thai name, and the digits of their birth date — day, month, and year. Each of these seven numbers falls between 1 and 9 (thanks to the base-9 reduction), and each maps to a specific domain of life.

Each number 1 through 9 carries a guardian planetary deity: 1 (Surya/Sun), 2 (Chandra/Moon), 3 (Angaraka/Mars), 4 (Rahu), 5 (Guru/Jupiter), 6 (Shukra/Venus), 7 (Ketu), 8 (Shani/Saturn), and 9 (Budha/Mercury). The interplay between your seven numbers — which planets are amplified, which are in tension, which are absent — forms the living portrait of your soul's design in this incarnation.

Crucially, the system is used prescriptively as well as descriptively. If a newborn's numbers reveal a weak Venus position, a name with Venus-resonant syllables may be selected to strengthen it. If an adult faces repeated obstacles, a Brahmin numerologist may suggest a legal name change to improve numerical harmony. The system believes the sounds we answer to shape the energy we carry.
